There is no single "passport size". The UK, the EU and India use 35 × 45 mm; the United States uses a 2 × 2 inch square; Canada uses a much larger 50 × 70 mm. Each also sets how big the head must be inside that frame, and which backgrounds are allowed. Get one of those wrong and the application comes back weeks later.

This guide lists the common sizes, explains the rules almost every country shares, and shows how to make a correct photo at home. The [Passport Size Photo Maker](/passport-photo-maker) has every size below built in: it finds the face, frames it to the right head height and lays copies out on a 4 × 6 inch or A4 sheet for printing.

> Rules change. Before you print, check the size against the official page for the passport or visa you are applying for — the table below is a starting point, not the authority.

## Sizes at a glance

| Country or document | Photo size | Head height (chin to crown) | Background |
|---|---|---|---|
| United Kingdom passport | 35 × 45 mm | 29–34 mm | Plain light grey or cream |
| Schengen / EU passports and visas | 35 × 45 mm | 32–36 mm | Plain, light |
| India ("passport size") | 35 × 45 mm | About 70–80 % of the height | Plain white or light |
| Russia passport | 35 × 45 mm | About 70–80 % of the height | Plain white or light |
| Australia passport | 35–40 × 45–50 mm | 32–36 mm | Plain, light |
| United States passport and visa | 2 × 2 in (51 × 51 mm) | 1–1⅜ in (25–35 mm) | White or off-white |
| Canada passport | 50 × 70 mm | 31–36 mm | White or light-coloured |
| China visa | 33 × 48 mm | 28–33 mm | White |
| Brazil and Indonesia documents | 3 × 4 cm | About 60 % of the height | Varies — check the form |

## Rules almost every country shares

Most passport authorities follow the ICAO standard for travel-document photos, so the same rules turn up everywhere:

- **Recent.** Usually taken within the last six months (the UK asks for the last month), and it must look like you now.
- **Face straight on.** Looking directly at the camera, head not tilted, both ears roughly visible.
- **Neutral expression.** Mouth closed, eyes open. A slight natural smile is accepted by some countries, not by others — neutral is always safe.
- **Plain background.** No pattern, no objects, no shadow of you on the wall behind.
- **Even light.** No shadows across the face, no bright reflection on the forehead or glasses.
- **Glasses.** The US does not allow them at all. Most other countries allow clear glasses with no glare and the eyes fully visible; taking them off avoids the problem.
- **No head covering** unless for religious or medical reasons, and then the face must be fully visible from chin to forehead.
- **Not edited.** Smoothing skin, removing blemishes or changing the face is not allowed. Cropping, straightening and changing a plain background to another plain colour are fine.

## Why head size matters most

Officers and automatic checks measure the head, not just the paper. A 35 × 45 mm photo where the face fills only half the height is rejected even though the paper size is right. The photo maker sets the head height for each size automatically from the detected face; if you crop by hand, measure from the chin to the top of the hair, not to the eyebrows.

## Digital photos for online applications

More and more applications are online, and they want a digital file rather than a print. These usually have rules in pixels and kilobytes rather than millimetres — for example, the UK online service asks for a photo at least 600 pixels wide and 750 pixels tall. Some forms also set a file-size limit; if yours does, see [How to make a photo exactly 50 KB](/blog/make-photo-exactly-50kb) or use [Compress Image to 50KB](/compress-image-to-50kb) after making the photo.

## Printing passport photos at home

1. Take the photo against a plain wall in daylight, with the camera at eye level about 1.5 metres away. Don't use the selfie camera at arm's length: it distorts the face.
2. Open the [Passport Size Photo Maker](/passport-photo-maker), choose your country's size and a plain background.
3. Download the print sheet (4 × 6 inch or A4) at 300 DPI.
4. Print it at **100 % / actual size** — not "fit to page", which shrinks the photos — on photo paper, or at any photo kiosk that prints 4 × 6 inch prints.
5. Cut along the guides. One 4 × 6 sheet holds several copies, so one print covers a family.

## Changing just the background

If you already have a good photo but the background is wrong — a coloured wall, or a document that needs white instead of blue — [Change Background Color](/change-background-color) replaces the background without touching the face.

## Quick answers

**Is "passport size" in India 35 × 45 mm?** Yes — that is the size forms, offices and photo studios in India mean by "passport size photo".

**Is a US passport photo the same as a US visa photo?** Yes, both are 2 × 2 inches with the same head-size and background rules.

**Can I use the same photo for two countries?** Only if both use the same size and rules — a UK and a Schengen photo are both 35 × 45 mm, but their head-height ranges differ slightly, so check both.

**What DPI should a passport photo be?** 300 DPI for printing. The print sheets from the photo maker are already at 300 DPI.
