Every platform squeezes your pictures into its own boxes. Upload a photo in the wrong shape and it gets cropped where you didn't want; upload one that is too small and it looks soft next to everyone else's. This page lists the sizes that work on each platform, what each box is for, and the few rules that keep a picture safe from awkward crops.

All of these sizes are built into [Resize Image](/resize-image): pick the platform and the box, and the photo is resized and cropped to fit. For the most common ones there are dedicated tools linked below.

> Platforms adjust their layouts often. The sizes here are what works well as of October 2026; when a platform recommends a size in its own help pages, that wins.

## Instagram

| Use | Size (px) | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Profile photo | 320 × 320 or larger (shown at 110 × 110) | 1:1, shown as a circle |
| Portrait post | 1080 × 1350 | 4:5 |
| Tall post (fills the profile grid) | 1080 × 1440 | 3:4 |
| Square post | 1080 × 1080 | 1:1 |
| Landscape post | 1080 × 566 | 1.91:1 |
| Story or Reel | 1080 × 1920 | 9:16 |

Since January 2025 the profile grid shows posts as 3:4 rectangles, and since May 2025 you can post 3:4 photos directly. A 3:4 post (1080 × 1440) is the only shape the grid shows uncropped; a 4:5 post loses a thin strip on each side there, and a square one loses more. To spread one big picture across your grid, use the [Instagram Grid Maker](/instagram-grid-maker).

For Stories and Reels, keep text and faces out of the top and bottom 250 pixels or so — the profile name, buttons and caption sit there.

## YouTube

| Use | Size (px) | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Thumbnail | 1280 × 720 | 16:9 |
| Channel banner | 2560 × 1440 | 16:9 |
| Channel icon | 800 × 800 | 1:1, shown as a circle |

The channel banner is shown very differently on TVs, desktops and phones. Only the centre **1546 × 423** pixels are visible everywhere, so keep the channel name and anything important inside that strip. Thumbnails should stay under 2 MB; [YouTube Thumbnail Resizer](/youtube-thumbnail-resizer) makes them 1280 × 720 JPGs.

## Facebook

| Use | Size (px) | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Profile photo | 170 × 170 or larger | 1:1, shown as a circle |
| Page or profile cover | 851 × 315 | about 2.7:1 |
| Shared link or post image | 1200 × 630 | 1.91:1 |
| Story | 1080 × 1920 | 9:16 |
| Event cover | 1920 × 1005 | about 1.91:1 |

Covers are cropped differently on phones, which show more height and less width, so keep text near the middle. [Facebook Cover Resizer](/facebook-cover-resizer) produces the 851 × 315 size.

## LinkedIn

| Use | Size (px) | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Profile photo | 400 × 400 | 1:1, shown as a circle |
| Profile cover (banner) | 1584 × 396 | 4:1 |
| Post image | 1200 × 627 | 1.91:1 |
| Company logo | 300 × 300 | 1:1 |

The profile photo overlaps the lower left of the banner, so don't put anything important there. Use [LinkedIn Banner Resizer](/linkedin-banner-resizer) for the 1584 × 396 banner.

## X (Twitter)

| Use | Size (px) | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Profile photo | 400 × 400 | 1:1, shown as a circle |
| Header | 1500 × 500 | 3:1 |
| Post image | 1600 × 900 | 16:9 |

## WhatsApp

| Use | Size (px) | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Profile photo (DP) | 500 × 500 | 1:1, shown as a circle |
| Status | 1080 × 1920 | 9:16 |

WhatsApp crops a profile photo to a circle and to a square in different places. [WhatsApp DP Resizer](/whatsapp-dp-resizer) fits the whole photo in without cutting anyone off.

## TikTok, Pinterest, Snapchat

| Platform | Use | Size (px) |
|---|---|---|
| TikTok | Profile photo | 200 × 200 or larger |
| TikTok | Video or Story cover | 1080 × 1920 |
| Pinterest | Standard pin | 1000 × 1500 (2:3) |
| Pinterest | Square pin | 1000 × 1000 |

## Discord

| Use | Size (px) |
|---|---|
| Profile picture | 128 × 128 or larger |
| Profile banner | 600 × 240 |
| Server banner | 960 × 540 |
| Server icon | 512 × 512 |

[Discord Banner Resizer](/discord-banner-resizer) handles the profile banner.

## Three rules that prevent bad crops

1. **Start bigger than you need.** A picture can always be scaled down sharply; scaling up looks soft. If a photo is too small for a banner, [Upscale Image](/upscale-image) or [Image to HD](/image-to-hd) can add resolution first.
2. **Mind the circle.** Profile photos are uploaded square but shown as circles, so the corners disappear. Keep the face centred with space around it. [Circle Crop](/circle-crop) shows you exactly what will be visible.
3. **Keep text away from the edges.** Banners and Stories are cropped differently on every screen. Put words and logos in the middle third.

## One photo, every platform

You don't have to edit a photo once per platform. Open it in [Resize Image](/resize-image), choose a platform preset, download, then choose the next — the original is kept, so each version is cut from the full photo rather than from the previous crop.
